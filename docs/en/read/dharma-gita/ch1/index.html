Chapter 1. The Chariot and the Battlefield

The Master said that life is a battlefield. The body is a chariot. The mind is the driver. The senses are the horses. And the soul is the Warrior sitting in the chariot.

Beside the soul stands an unseen Friend, who guides it along the path of Dharma.

The chariot, pulled by the horses of the senses and steered by the mind, rides across the great field of this world.

Along the way, it meets friends and enemies. Arrows of doubt fly past. Swords of passion flash. Thick, tangled thoughts suddenly block the road like fallen trees.

The road splits, then joins again. It turns, it loops back. The speed keeps changing too: sometimes the chariot jolts forward, sometimes it flies like the wind, and sometimes it gets stuck in a swamp, unable to move.

But the field of life is not chaos. Like in any great game, it has its own rules.

The Law of the Soul's Eternal Nature

The Master said our chariot — the body — is born and grows old. But the Warrior never does.

A wise man from the West once said that energy never comes from nothing and never disappears — it only changes form. In the same way, the soul leaves one chariot after some time, and moves into another. It gets a new chariot based on how well it drove the old one.

The Law of Karma, or Cause and Effect

Every action creates a reaction. Every thought casts a shadow. Every choice will bring results in time.

This is not punishment.

These are simply the rules of the map of this field. Whoever knows them can read the roads ahead.

The Law of Skillful Action

The Master called this "The Way of the Chariot."

He said that only those who can master the five horses of the senses truly control their own fate.

The mind makes a good servant, but a bad master. A warrior who blindly trusts his mind will lose control — the horses will overturn his chariot. You must rule the mind, not obey it. This takes a strong mind and a strong will.

Outer action is only half the skill.

The other half is inner intention.

Someone who acts not for reward, not for praise or wealth, and not out of fear of losing something, but simply to stay true to the right path — this person wins the battle, even with strong enemies and a dull sword.
