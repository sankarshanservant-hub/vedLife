Chapter 3. Jnana Yoga

Who Is a True Wise Person

The monks told how, when students asked the Great Teacher what it means to be wise in a world full of battles and change, he pointed to a boat on the riverbank and said:

"Life is a river. Sometimes it rages, sometimes it's calm. Sometimes it sweeps the boat along fast, sometimes it gives you time to wait. But its goal never changes — to join the ocean."

Here, the river is time. The boat is the body.

To continue this picture: the mind is the driver holding the reins of the body's senses. The soul is the warrior in the chariot.

Sometimes the chariot rides across a dusty battlefield. Sometimes it stands quietly on the shore. Sometimes it floats down the river. But for the wise, all these paths are just one Path.

The Wise Always Move Toward the Highest Goal

The Teacher said that for an ordinary person, every road looks different. But for a wise person, every road leads upward. Such a person doesn't cling to the twists of fate, doesn't fear rough waters, doesn't grieve in a drought, and doesn't boast about good luck. He knows the river must reach the Ocean. However many bends there are, the final goal never changes.

The Wise Act, But Not for Reward

On the battlefield of life, people fall into three kinds: those who act for reward, those who act out of fear, and those who act for the path itself. Only the last kind find freedom.

The Master said that whoever works for the reward becomes a slave to the outcome. But whoever acts for the path becomes the master of his own fate. Rewards come to the wise on their own, the way a harvest comes to someone who works the land not for gold, but out of love for the land itself.

For the wise, nothing is great or small. A king and a dog, a scholar and a beggar all carry the same spark of life inside them. The old Taoists said that bodies differ, but the breath of Tao is one:

"If you see differences, you're looking through the eyes of the body.

If you see oneness, you're looking through the eyes of the soul."

The wise don't see skin, clothes, names, or roles. They see the one sitting in the chariot, and in everyone they recognize the same Traveler, heading to the same Ocean.

The Wise Stay Equal in Joy and Pain

On the field of life, some win honors, others suffer defeat. Some find treasure, others lose it. But the wise stay steady. They know that neither success nor failure touches the soul — only the chariot.

So they walk on evenly: not soaring in joy, not falling in pain, not proud in victory, not broken in defeat. Their mind stays as still as a lake at dawn. In that stillness, the Sky is reflected.

The Wise Know Their True Nature

The Teacher said that real knowledge means knowing what you are not, and who you have always been. He knows he is not the chariot, because it doesn't last. Not the mind, because it keeps changing. Not the senses, because they flare up and fade. Not the role he plays in this world, because it's only temporary. He is the eternal Traveler, who came from a world of light. The body is a boat on the river, but he is not the boat. He is the one holding the oar.

The River Must Reach the Ocean

The Teacher taught that no matter how much a river bends, it was born to reach the sea. In the same way, the soul was born to return to its Source. If

the wise understand that they are immortal, that their true home lies beyond the body, that they came from a world of love and light and will return there again — then, even while alive, they become immortal. Not because the body won't die, but because they stop seeing themselves as the body.

A True Wise Person Is One Who Has Woken Up

Finally, the Teacher said that a wise person is not someone who knows a lot, but someone who knows himself. Not someone who defeated his enemies, but someone who mastered his own mind. Not someone who reached the end of the road, but someone who understood where the road leads.

And then the chariot becomes just a tool, life becomes a path, the river becomes movement, and the Ocean becomes the home the soul came from and must return to.
