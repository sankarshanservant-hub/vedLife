Chapter 2. True Knowledge

The Master said there are different levels of knowledge.

There is knowledge about the field itself. There is knowledge about how the chariot works. There is knowledge of the laws of motion, and how to master the senses and the mind. But the highest knowledge is knowledge of the Warrior. Who is he, and where is he going in his chariot? What is our soul? What are we, at our deepest core?

A person could study the field of this world forever: the stars, the plants, numbers, how people behave, the patterns behind historical events...

But until someone knows themselves, they will wander among shapes and tempting illusions, like a traveler lost among countless shadows.

The Difference Between the Chariot and the Warrior

A chariot can be strong or weak, fast or slow, beautiful and costly or broken and barely moving. But it's not the soul. It's not you.

Our senses are the horses. Quick, moody, stubborn, always pulling us toward things we want. They are almost always strong. But they're not you either.

The mind can be clear or clouded, sharp or lazy, brave or scared. It may handle the horses of the senses well or badly, stay firm or get pulled along. But it's not you either.

The real You is the one sitting in the chariot. The one who guides the mind and senses. The one who chooses the road.

A New Level Means a New Chariot

The Teacher taught that life is a great game with countless levels, and the field keeps changing. On each level, the rules stay almost the same. But you, the player, get a new chariot and a new world to understand — a new field. When a level ends, whether it went well or badly, the time comes to leave the chariot behind and move on.

The Goal of the Game Is to Wake Up

As an old master once said, many roads lead to one peak. Many battles lead to one Victory.

The goal of this great game is to win the battle of our life. But this win doesn't mean destroying enemies. It means winning over not knowing.

The real win is leaving the game. Whoever manages this is the true Winner.

Three States of Being

The Teacher said that the same actions can bring different results, because they come from different states of mind.

He named three states:

Ignorance — when the mind is foggy and lazy. This is night, when the chariot loses its way and gets stuck in a swamp of doing nothing and dark wants that lead only to a cliff's edge.

Passion — when the mind craves the fruits of its work, and pushes hard only to get them. This is the heat of noon. This is fire that burns up both the chariot and its driver, scorching its own owner.

Goodness — when the mind is clear and calm. This is morning, when the road can be seen far ahead, the driver is clear-headed, and the horses obey their rider's will.

These three states mix together, painting a rich array of colors on the canvas of our fate.
