Introduction

A student asked the Master:

"What will you teach us? Who will your words help, and how? Why do we need this knowledge?"

The Master bowed humbly and answered:

"I will share the Dharma Gita with you. Some call it 'The Song of the Way.' This book belongs to no land, no country. It was not born in any single place. It rose up in my heart."

This knowledge is a gift. It came through the grace of many wise people I met on my long travels.

"The Song of the Way" belongs to no religion. But it holds the heart of every religion. This knowledge never fades. It has nothing to do with matter. What the heart comes to know leads to what never dies.

"The Song of the Way" has no end. Because the Way itself, where this book comes from, has no beginning and no end. It lasts forever, and it leads only to what lasts forever.

The Dharma Gita can speak to anyone. It can help in any situation in life.

But each person will find something different in it:

Someone who wants to live a good life will grow stronger in virtue. They will find stability, wealth, and respect. In time, they may also let go of everything they don't need.

Someone who seeks knowledge will gain more than facts. They will gain real understanding — an inner experience beyond the mind and the senses.

Someone who wants to let go of the world and live alone will find joy in quiet reflection on this knowledge, deep in their heart.

And someone who wants to share it with others will see that many hearts thirst for the Truth.

This is the good that can come from what I want to tell you.

In its deepest sense, this knowledge is for those who walk the path of the heart, the path of love. But it will also help someone who has just begun to look for their own way.

The Way is one, like a staircase leading to Heaven. But it has many steps. Each person will see this knowledge from the step where they stand. And it will help them climb to the next one.

But let the seeker remember: the Way never ends. Each step on this staircase to Truth leads not only upward, but also deeper into the heart.

A wise person values time. They won't stay too long trying to reach the full depth of every step. Once they understand one step, they move on. They know that moving through these steps is itself moving upward.

Still, the depth of each step matters too. Without it, a person could stumble and fall.

This is how a wise person moves: not rushing, but not lingering either — always listening to the knowledge.

"The Song of the Way" has no limit — not in height, not in depth, not in width.

It is like the sky.

And in this sky, the Sun of Love always shines, lighting up the soul and its Source.

Blessed be the one who reads this book. May they light a star in their heart, one that reflects the pure, divine light of the highest knowledge — the knowledge of universal, divine love.
